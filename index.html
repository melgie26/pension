🛡️ Pension Deduction Rules

💡 No CPP or EI Premiums on Pension Income:

CPP and EI premiums are never deducted from passive retirement income, including CPP benefits, OAS, GIS, or private/corporate pension payouts.

If You Choose to Work While Collecting:

CPP (Age 60–65):

Deductions from employment earnings are mandatory and build a Post-Retirement Benefit (PRB).

CPP (Age 65–70):

Voluntary. You can opt out of CPP deductions on earnings by filing CRA form CPT30.

🔄 Inflation Shields & Indexing Safeguards

🛡️ LAPP Pension COLA Annual (Jan)

LAPP pensions normally receive a guaranteed annual adjustment equal to 60% of the change in Alberta CPI. For January 2026, LAPP approved a one-time enhancement to 90% of CPI, producing a 1.8% increase.

📈 CPP Indexing Protection Annual (Jan)

Canada Pension Plan payouts are automatically indexed every January matching CPI shifts.

⚖️ OAS Quarterly Adjustments Quarterly

Old Age Security (OAS) benefits are indexed quarterly (Jan, Apr, Jul, Oct) to respond to cost of living changes.

Federal Basic Personal Amount (BPA) Logic

2026 Income Tax Exemption Threshold & Non-Refundable Credit Rules

Est. Federal BPA Based on Active Income $16,452.00
🛡️ Tax-Free Earnings Ceiling

The Federal Basic Personal Amount allows every taxpayer to earn income tax-free up to the federal BPA threshold before income tax mathematically applies.

💵 Non-Refundable Tax Credit

Calculated at the 14% lowest federal tax bracket rate, your calculated BPA yields a maximum non-refundable federal tax credit reduction of $2,303.28.

📉 High Earner Clawback Phase-Out

For high-income earners with net income between $181,440 and $258,482, the BPA is gradually clawed back until it reaches the minimum threshold of $14,829.
